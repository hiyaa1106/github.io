Our Heritage

The Culture of Cloth

Where centuries of Indian weaving tradition meet the precision of modern textile craft.

01

Surat is not merely a city — it is a living, breathing loom. For over six centuries, this port city on the banks of the Tapi River has been the epicentre of India's textile trade, weaving prosperity from thread and cloth into the fabric of global commerce.

At Vardhman Textiles, we are rooted in this legacy. With over 25 years of craft experience, our atelier in Surat brings together master weavers, traditional artisans, and modern design sensibilities to create fabrics that carry the soul of Indian heritage.

From the intricate zari work of Gujarati bridal wear to the flowing grace of georgette sarees — every metre of fabric leaving our looms carries a story of craft, care, and cultural pride.

Our commitment is simple: to honour the traditions of the past while creating textiles worthy of the world's most discerning designers and buyers.

Where Ancient Craft Meets Modern Precision

The loom has been the heartbeat of Surat for generations. Today, Vardhman Textiles brings this living tradition into the modern era — not by replacing the artisan's hand, but by empowering it with precision tools and global design insights.

Our production facility integrates computer-aided design with traditional weaving techniques. Master artisans and digital designers work in the same space, creating fabrics that carry both the warmth of handcraft and the consistency of technology.

The result is a fabric collection that can satisfy the exacting demands of international fashion houses while staying true to the cultural soul of Indian textile arts.

Gujarati Tradition

Bandhani

The Art of Tie-Dye

Born in the lanes of Kutch and Jamnagar, Bandhani is one of the oldest tie-dye traditions in the world. Artisans pinch tiny portions of cloth and bind them with thread before dyeing, creating intricate patterns of dots, waves, and geometric forms. A single saree can require thousands of individually tied knots — each a testament to extraordinary patience and skill. Vardhman celebrates this tradition through our curated Bandhani collection.

02
Double Ikat Weave

Patola

The Royal Weave

Patola sarees from Patan, Gujarat are among the most technically complex textiles in the world. Using the double ikat technique, both the warp and weft threads are resist-dyed before weaving — requiring that every thread land in exactly the right position for the pattern to emerge. Historically gifted only to royalty and temple deities, Patola represents the absolute pinnacle of Indian weaving mastery. We honour this legacy with our Patola-inspired woven collection.

Thread Stories

Gujarati Embroidery

Mirror Work & Beyond

Gujarat's embroidery traditions are as diverse as its communities. Rabari mirror work embeds tiny shisha glass into geometric patterns. Kutchi embroidery uses chain stitch and herringbone to tell stories of flora and fauna. Ahir embroidery vibrates with bold geometric energy. Each community carries its own distinct vocabulary of thread, needle, and colour — passed down through generations of women who embroider their lives into cloth. Our embroidery fabrics celebrate this living oral history.

04
Gold in Thread

Zari Work

Metal Thread Weaving

Zari is the art of weaving fine metallic thread — traditionally real gold and silver — into fabric to create luminous, intricate patterns. Surat has been the heartland of zari production in India for centuries, supplying the golden borders of bridal sarees, the shimmering pallus of festival silks, and the ceremonial brocades of royal courts. Today, Vardhman carries this tradition forward with a range of authentic zari-embellished fabrics that bring the warmth of gold thread to contemporary fashion.

From Surat to the World.
